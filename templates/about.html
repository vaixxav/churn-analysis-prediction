About this project

Why churn, and why this way

Customer churn is one of the few ML problems where the business case writes itself: it's cheaper to keep a customer than to acquire a new one, and a probability score is only useful if someone can act on it. RetentionIQ is built around that — every prediction ships with a reason, not just a number.

The data

Trained on the IBM Telco Customer Churn dataset (~7,000 accounts). After dropping the customer ID, coercing TotalCharges to numeric, and removing a handful of rows with missing values, the data was label-encoded for binary fields and one-hot encoded (with the first category dropped) for the rest — 30 features in total.

The model

Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and SVM were all trained and evaluated on the same 80/20 split. Logistic Regression was deployed: it has the best ROC AUC (0.852) and recall of the three, and as a linear model its coefficients are easy to reason about when explaining a prediction.
